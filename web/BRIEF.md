## The case
The VP of Customer Experience says flight disruption and inconsistent service are hurting the airline and asks for a real-time operations dashboard. The Director of Data Science says the data team is overloaded. The CIO wants a recommendation and a credible pilot. Vendor Aero-Lytics offers a dashboard that deploys quickly. Case title: "The dashboard that arrives too late."

Decision: prioritise operational interventions **before** disruption. Target: `disruption_next_hour` (1 = disruption). Features: x1 queue depth (flights), x2 weather severity (0–1), x3 turnaround buffer (minutes). Records are flight-hours keyed `segment:entity_id` (segment = region north/south/east/west). Splits by time: 1,200 train, 400 validation, 400 blind.

What discovery (customer Q&A rounds) told us (these are customer answers, `data.business`):
- Data freshness latency today is 10+ minutes; ops needs under 1 minute to act. Consequence: delays compound, crew decisions arrive too late.
- Root cause per customer: fragmented legacy architecture and an overwhelmed data team.
- About 14 operational delays per week today; target is a 50% reduction.
- Three user groups: Operations (day-to-day, need flight status, issues, predictions, decision support inside a sub-30-minute window); Data analytics team (data quality, lineage, structure: they use Databricks and Genie directly, no dashboard needed); CIO (aggregated summary, revenue and cost impact, defensible to the C-suite).
- The CIO is the ultimate decision maker. The data team assesses and tests proposed solutions.
- Buying Aero-Lytics was judged insufficient: it does not address upstream data latency.

Our diagnosis: the request is "buy a dashboard"; the problem is that signals are late, revised and inconsistent at decision time, so any dashboard shows wrong numbers faster. Fix the signal first (point-in-time, keyed, unit-normalised, permitted), then put two lanes on top: an Ops desk (ranked next-hour risk queue sized to capacity) and a CIO view (impact, evidence, next gate). The data team gets Genie, not a dashboard.

## Honesty rules (the jury checks these)
- The model predicts next-hour disruption risk. It does NOT show that an intervention prevents a disruption. Any "delays avoided" or dollar figure is a scenario on explicit, editable, labelled assumptions (`assumptions`), never a result.
- Validation figures are the only performance figures. Never present blind accuracy or imply a blind outcome is known.
- `data.business` numbers are customer answers from discovery, not measured; say "from discovery".
- Do not conflate the customer's 10-minute freshness latency with our measured late revisions (24 h after decision time). Both support "signals arrive too late", but they are different measurements.
- Say nothing about the real JetBlue. Footer exists. No logos.
- `resolved_outcome_hint` appears only as a leakage test.
- Only numbers from `data` or computed from it. Unknown means open question. Never print a group UUID or any email.
- Copy: plain, short, no marketing language, no emojis, no em dashes. Words: "flag for intervention" / "monitor", "flight-hour", "disruption", not "positive class".
