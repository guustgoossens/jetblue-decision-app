## Experiment contract (verbatim for `lib/experiment.ts`)
1. Business decision: "Before disruption hits, rank the upcoming flight-hours by next-hour disruption risk and send interventions down that list, up to what the ops desk can handle. Do this before buying a dashboard (Aero-Lytics), because a dashboard on the current feed would show late, revised data."
2. Data grain: "One row per expected flight-hour (segment:entity_id at its decision time). Each row uses only data that was available by that time and permitted for model training. 2,000 expected records: 1,707 usable, 293 quarantined with a reason."
3. Metric definition: "Model: balanced accuracy on disruption_next_hour (1 = disruption), at a 0.50 cut-off. Business: operational delays per week at the pilot hub."
4. Baseline: "Model: the Dummy scores 0.500 balanced accuracy. Business (from discovery): about 14 delays per week; data freshness latency is 10+ minutes."
5. Target: "Model: blind balanced accuracy of at least 0.70 and at least Dummy + 0.05. Our champion scores 0.917 on validation. Business: 50% fewer delays (about 7 fewer per week), and data that arrives in under 1 minute."
6. Unit: "Balanced accuracy (0 to 1); delays per week; minutes of data latency."
7. Population / denominator / time window: "Validation: 342 usable flight-hours out of 400. Blind: 342 out of 400, from 3 Sep 2026, 02:40 to 09:19 UTC. The denominator stays 2,000, quarantined records included. Pilot: delays per week at one hub over 6 to 8 weeks, compared with a matched control hub."
8. Measurement method: "Logistic regression trained on the train split only (1,023 records), chosen in MLflow on validation balanced accuracy against the Dummy, a random forest and gradient boosting. The blind set is scored by the jury. Pilot: count delays per week and interventions started before the disruption, at the pilot hub and at the control hub."
9. What would falsify the diagnosis?: "Ops already gets correct data in under a minute and delays continue anyway. Or the repaired, on-time data does not beat Dummy + 0.05 on the blind set. Or the pilot hub shows no fewer delays than the control hub, or duty managers stop trusting the alerts because of false alarms. Any of these means the problem is not late or untrustworthy signals."
10. What can this experiment not establish?: "That intervening prevents disruptions: the model predicts risk, it does not show cause. How fast the real production feed is. Anything about the real JetBlue, because the data is synthetic. Results across the network, from a one-hub pilot."
11. Unresolved quality / access prerequisite: "171 records are not permitted for model training at decision time, so the data likely needs a privacy and legal review. 67 queue-depth values cannot be parsed and cannot be repaired. 55 flight-hours had no observation in time. The app's own service account lacks catalog access for Genie (needs a USE CATALOG grant on xhec26). Production data latency has not been measured; the 10+ minutes is the customer's figure."

## Review (verbatim, split into the 4 parts)
Observed: "On validation, the champion logistic regression scores 0.917 balanced accuracy, against 0.500 for the Dummy. It uses only queue depth, weather severity and turnaround buffer, all repaired so they reflect what was known at decision time. At a 0.50 cut-off it flags 157 flight-hours, and 144 of those were real disruptions. The 25% riskiest flight-hours saw a 93% disruption rate; the 25% least risky saw 1.2%. The repair mattered: joining on raw keys loses 703 of 2,000 records, and 216 revisions only arrived about 24 hours after the decision."
Implication: "The problem is late, untrustworthy data, not a missing screen. Ops can act on a short ranked list if the data is fixed first, and a dashboard on the current data would show wrong numbers faster."
Limitation: "The data is synthetic, blind accuracy stays hidden until the jury grades, the model predicts risk without showing that intervening prevents a disruption, and the 10-minute latency is the customer's figure, not our measurement."
Next test: "A 6 to 8 week pilot at one hub using the repaired data and a ranked list sized to the desk's capacity. Count delays per week (baseline about 14, target 50% fewer) and interventions started before the disruption, compared with a matched control hub. Duty managers' tolerance for false alarms is the stop condition."

## The case
The VP of Customer Experience says flight disruption and inconsistent service are hurting the airline and asks for a real-time operations dashboard. The Director of Data Science says the data team is overloaded. The CIO wants a recommendation and a credible pilot. Vendor Aero-Lytics offers a dashboard that deploys quickly. Case title: "The dashboard that arrives too late."

Decision: prioritise operational interventions **before** disruption. Target: `disruption_next_hour` (1 = disruption). Features: x1 queue depth (flights), x2 weather severity (0–1), x3 turnaround buffer (minutes). Records are flight-hours keyed `segment:entity_id` (segment = region north/south/east/west). - Data freshness latency today is 10+ minutes; ops needs under 1 minute to act. Consequence: delays compound, crew decisions arrive too late.

- Buying Aero-Lytics was judged insufficient: it does not address upstream data latency.

Our diagnosis: the request is "buy a dashboard"; the problem is that signals are late, revised and inconsistent at decision time, so any dashboard shows wrong numbers faster. Fix the signal first (point-in-time, keyed, unit-normalised, permitted), then put two lanes on top: an Ops desk (ranked next-hour risk queue sized to capacity) and a CIO view (impact, evidence, next gate).
